# StrongShock: Lean 4 formalization

Supplementary material for the manuscript *Spectral stability for strong ideal-gas shock layers*.
Section 11 of the manuscript describes the formalization. Numbers below refer to the manuscript.

The formalization depends only on the Lean 4 core and Mathlib. It is archived at Zenodo,
[doi:10.5281/zenodo.23288169](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23288169).

* Lean toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1` (file `lean-toolchain`)
* Mathlib `v4.34.1`, commit `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612` (files `lakefile.toml`, `lake-manifest.json`)

## Building

With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed,

```
lake exe cache get     # download prebuilt Mathlib
lake build             # check every proof in this project
lake env lean Check.lean
```

The last command prints the axioms used by each main result. Every one of them depends only on
`propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, the standard axioms of Lean and Mathlib. The
sources contain no `sorry`, `admit`, or `axiom`. A full build takes about five minutes on two
cores once the Mathlib cache is in place. A transcript of such a run is in `verification_log.txt`.

The notebook `StrongShock_Lean_verification_colab.ipynb` in this folder performs
the same steps on Google Colab without any local setup.

## Results used as hypotheses

The following results of Humpherys, Lyng, and Zumbrun (Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 194, 2009), or
standard facts about ordinary differential equations, are not available in Mathlib. They appear as
explicit hypotheses of the theorems and never as axioms.

1. `LimitingProfile`. A solution of (2.3) between the endstates, with the endstate limits and a
   positive downstream limit of the normalized slope `z = -V'/(a s)`. The file `Becker.lean` shows
   that this structure is inhabited.
2. `Admissible`. For nonzero spectral parameter, decay `O(e^{ωx})` upstream for every `ω < 1/ν`,
   and decay downstream.
3. `ZeroAdmissible`. At zero frequency, decay upstream, and downstream limits in the
   positive-characteristic subspace with decaying derivatives.
4. `VariationalUnique` and `AllVariationalUnique`. Downstream decaying solutions of the variational
   equation (9.1) are multiples of the profile tangent.
5. For finite shocks, an integrated Evans function `D(λ, (Γ, ν), ς)` that is jointly continuous
   through `ς = 0`, satisfies a uniform high-frequency bound, and whose zeros in the closed right
   half-plane at `ς = 0` produce nontrivial admissible solutions (`EvansZeroSolution`).

## Polynomial inequalities

After the substitutions `ν = 1 + Γk` and `z = 1/(1 + Γ(1 + (k - 1)τ))`, each scalar inequality of
Lemmas 6.1 and 7.1 becomes nonnegativity of a polynomial on a box in `(Γ, k, τ, s)`. The files in
`Bernstein/` prove this by an exact expansion in the tensor Bernstein basis whose coefficients are
nonnegative, after at most one bisection. The files in `Bridge/` prove that each polynomial equals
the corresponding energy coefficient from `Basic.lean` after clearing positive denominators. To
regenerate these files and compare them with the supplied ones,

```
mkdir -p /tmp/regen/StrongShock
SS_ROOT=/tmp/regen python3 tools/gen_all.py
diff -r /tmp/regen/StrongShock/Bernstein StrongShock/Bernstein
diff -r /tmp/regen/StrongShock/Bridge StrongShock/Bridge
```

The generator uses only the Python standard library and exact rational arithmetic.

## Differences from the written proofs

* The scalar inequalities of Lemmas 6.1 and 7.1 are proved by the Bernstein expansions above rather
  than by the monotonicity reductions in the text. The bounds are the ones stated in the manuscript.
  For the coefficient `C`, the expansions bound the two functions of (7.5)-(7.6), and `C_decomp`
  verifies the decomposition (7.4).
* The exclusion of nonzero frequencies shows that the energy flux is nonincreasing with zero limits
  at both ends, which forces it to vanish identically.
* Lemma 2.2 is proved for every `ν > 0`.
